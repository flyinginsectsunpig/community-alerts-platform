# ECHOES — Worldbuilding: The City of Carillon

The setting bible. The single most important section is §1.3 — the metaphysics rulebook that every other doc leans on.

---

## 1. The world's physics (read first)

### 1.1 The Meridian

At the bottom of a drowned sea-chasm there is a seam where all hours touch: **the Meridian**. Time near it is *frayed* — days repeat, memories slip, bodies age wrong. Time far from it runs clean.

### 1.2 Clean time rises

The load-bearing conceit: **tuned time is buoyant**. Bells cast from meridian-touched bronze can "ring time upward" — gather frayed hours at the bottom, refine them, and send the clean remainder up the city's spine. This is why Carillon is vertical, why the rich live high and age slowly, why the poor live low and age fast, and why the whole city is functionally a distillery with people living in the pipework. Height = temporal purity = class. The architecture is the political system is the mechanic.

### 1.3 The three shards (metaphysics rulebook)

Sixty-one years ago the Null Chime cracked the city's hour into three shards:

- **The Eve** — the day before the Red Ascension, preserved. Golden light, intact streets, inhabitants looping their last untroubled day. Not "the past": a *kept* fragment.
- **The Now** — the surviving timeline. Ash, rationing, the Order's winding. The only shard where people truly live, change, and die.
- **The Hush** — the Null Chime's completion, still slowly arriving. A city mid-unmaking: silence, white fog, geometry dissolving. It rises from the Fathom like a floodline. The poor drown in it first.

**Rules (non-negotiable, they keep the game buildable and the fiction coherent):**
1. The shards are **parallel, not sequential**. The Eve does not cause the Now. Nothing done in one shard propagates to another…
2. …except **resonant matter** (meridian-touched bell-metal), which exists identically in all three shards at once. Move it in one, it moves in all. Bells are resonant; so are certain doors, braziers, and the Anchorite's chains.
3. **Living people exist in one shard only.** Eve-figures are loops, not people (morally load-bearing for the endings). The exceptions are unstuck beings: the player, Ambrose (mind only), the Anchorite, and Remnants.
4. The Hush **rises** at story beats, not in real time. Ground floor by ground floor, the lowest city is being quietly abandoned while the Order insists otherwise.

## 2. The city, bottom to top

Carillon accretes around the **Spine** — the original bell-tower, the only structure identical in all three shards — like coral around a mast. Six districts. Each entry notes its dressing in all three states, because that's the unit of level design.

### 2.1 The Fathom *(tutorial + endgame return)*

The drowned foundations: the first city, sunk parish by parish as Carillon climbed off its own past. Black water, leaning steeples, the Meridian's antechamber.
- **Eve:** shallow water, candle-lit vigils, the old parishes still half-alive.
- **Now:** flooded and abandoned; Choir pilgrims wade down, Stitchers cache contraband.
- **Hush:** the floodline itself — the fog sits *on* the water here. Origin point.
- **Custodian:** **The Bailiff of the Drowned Parish** — the tutorial boss, a warden who still collects tithes from empty pews.

### 2.2 The Tolls *(first real district)*

Lowest inhabited ring. Fray-sick tenements stacked on old flood-walls; a day here sometimes runs twice. Named for what it pays and what it hears.
- **Eve:** market-day forever — the same bread sold to the same hands each loop.
- **Now:** rationed, gray, evacuating downward-facing floors as the Hush rises; Choir recruiters work the grief.
- **Hush:** the lower Tolls are already *in* it.
- **Custodian:** **The Toll-Reeve** — the Order's tithe-engine, still weighing hours nobody has.

### 2.3 The Weld *(vertical slice district)*

The industrial belt that holds the upper city onto the lower — foundries, chain-yards, counterweight elevators, and the **Long Forge** where every bell in Carillon was cast. Stitchguild territory. Outside its skin cling the **Spurs**: scaffold-slums with no floors at all, all ladders and rope — the traversal playground.
- **Eve:** the forges roar at full song; the district is *proud*.
- **Now:** half-cold forges kept alight to prove a point; Guild law beneath Order law.
- **Hush:** the great chains hang slack into the fog and do not end.
- **Custodian:** **Cast-Mother Sefa, the Unstruck Bell** (see [01](01-game-design-document.md) §4.7).

### 2.4 Clockrow *(middle city)*

Respectable poverty and total bureaucracy. The **Census of Hours** — the ledger of every citizen's temporal account — and the watch-houses of the **Escapement**. Permits, queues, informants.
- **Eve:** the Census the day before it burned; clerks looping their final filings.
- **Now:** rebuilt Census, twice the clerks, half the truth.
- **Hush:** corridors of blank paper; the fog reads like redaction.
- **Custodian:** **The Census Engine** — the arena-metronome boss.

### 2.5 The Gilt Terraces

Hanging gardens, private chapels with personal bells, banked centuries. Terrace-folk age a year in ten; their children are rare and treasured and strange. The Order's **Residency** scheme (see side thread 1, [03](03-narrative.md)) sells the wealthy passage *into the Eve itself* — emigration into nostalgia.
- **Eve:** indistinguishable from the Now at first glance — the point, and the tell.
- **Now:** lovely and terrified; the Hush is an abstraction here, and the abstraction is buying property.
- **Hush:** the gardens hold their shape in the fog longer than anything else in the city. Wealth's last privilege: dissolving slowly.
- **Custodian:** **The Gilded Twins** — the cross-state duo boss.

### 2.6 The Lantern *(endgame)*

The Order's crown: the Great Carillon, the Horologium seminary, the **Ossuary of Unstruck Hours** (where the Order keeps everything it has ever erased — see side thread 3), and at the very top, the **Anchorite's Cell** and the cracked **Null Chime**.
- All three shards converge here; near the Spine's crown the states bleed and the level design goes deliberately strange.
- **Custodians:** **Sister Vesper** (if unresolved), then **the Anchorite**.

## 3. Factions

| Faction | Seat | In one line | Player relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Winding Order** | The Lantern | Priest-engineers who wind the city and ration its hours; authors of the official history and the Null Chime. | Primary antagonist institution — but full of true believers, not cartoon villains. |
| **The Escapement** | Clockrow | The Order's militant regulators; hunt temporal deviants ("misfiled souls" — you). Named for the clock part that *permits* motion, one tick at a time. | Recurring elite enemies; Vesper's order. |
| **The Gilded Assembly** | Terraces | Aristocrats who buy clean hours and are now quietly buying the past itself. | Quest-givers, moral mirror, Residency thread. |
| **The Stitchguild** | The Weld | Outlawed guild of slip-runners — smugglers who map fissures and move goods and people between hours. Practical, mercenary, indispensable. | Ally-of-convenience; source of slip upgrades and the Great Slip thread. |
| **The Sundered Choir** | The Tolls / Fathom | Cult that worships the Hush as mercy — *"the quiet is kind"* — and opens doors to hasten it. Led by the Threnodist. | Enemies with the best arguments in the game. |
| **Remnants** | Nowhere | Not a faction: the erased. People and orders unmade by chimes past, persisting as echoes only the Unhoured perceives clearly. | Enemy ecology + the game's most tragic NPCs (Hollow-of-Bells). |

## 4. History (as the player can excavate it)

The official history is a lie with good posture; the true sequence is recoverable through item lore, the Vigil thread, and the Ossuary.

1. **The Wellfound Age.** A fisher-village on a chasm where nets came up with yesterday's fish. Discovery of the Meridian; first meridian-bronze bell cast to *tune* the fray. The **Vigil of the Ford** founded to guard the seam — the order history forgot, because it was made to forget (see thread 3).
2. **The Concordance.** The Winding Order codifies the art: bells ring time upward. The city begins to climb. The Vigil objects to treating the seam as a well. The Vigil, abruptly, has never existed — **the first chime the Order will not admit to.**
3. **The Long Ascent.** Centuries of building. The Census of Hours; time-tithes; the low aging fast so the high age slow. Carillon perfects the machine these documents describe.
4. **The Red Ascension.** The Weld and the Tolls rise — not for coin: *"We ask the hours back."* They breach Clockrow and burn the Census. The Assembly panics; the Order chooses erasure over defeat.
5. **The Fracture (year 0; game begins in year 61).** The Null Chime is rung — by a lowborn apprentice's hand, because the Meridian answers only those steeped in fray (the Order's clean-handed masters literally cannot touch their own weapon; the cruelty is structural). The chime miscarries. The hour shatters into three. The bell-ringer is erased as a loose end.
6. **The Waking Years.** Rationing. The official story: *the rebels broke the sky.* The Hush rises. The Order winds the city and waits for a solution it privately knows is a person: an anchor.

## 5. Daily life texture (for env art and incidental writing)

- Wages paid in stamped **hour-chits**; a Tolls laborer's week can be *docked to yesterday* — everyone hates re-living a docked day, everyone has done it.
- Grief works differently when the Eve exists: the Order sells **viewing-hours** — supervised minutes watching your dead loop through their last good day. Entire families ruin themselves on it. (Mara's wound; see [04](04-characters.md).)
- Bells structure everything: work-bells, curfew-bells, the Vespers. A city that flinches when a bell is *almost* rung.
- Fray-sickness: the low-city's signature — grey streaks by thirty, "stutters" (see combat status), sufferers called *the twice-told*.
- Escapement checkpoints between districts demand **clean papers** — proof your personal hour is tithed and tuned. The player never has clean papers. Every ascent is trespass.
