# ECHOES — Characters

Protagonist + six key NPCs. The design unit here is the **cross-state presentation table**: what each character is in the Eve / Now / Hush, since that's where this cast differs from a standard RPG roster. Voice samples in [03-narrative](03-narrative.md) §3.

**Casting rule (scope-aware):** each NPC has exactly one *"how they exist across states"* gimmick, and the roster covers the full possibility space between them — all-three-states (Ambrose), one-per-state-different-selves (Vesper), single-state-only (Mara), unstuck-upward (Orven), no-state (Hollow), all-states-at-once (Anchorite). The cast *is* the mechanic's tutorial.

---

## 0. The Unhoured (protagonist)

- **Who:** The apprentice who rang the Null Chime — coerced, then erased. Not amnesiac: *unwritten*. There is nothing to remember; the game is the act of being re-written. Player-customizable body; silent apart from breath and effort (soulslike convention, also the honest reading: the unwritten have no lines yet).
- **The shard:** cracked bell-metal fused in the sternum — visible in every armor set (armor design constraint, worth it). It is the slip mechanic, the plot, and the HUD's diegetic anchor in one object.
- **Across states:** the only being who moves freely between all three. Eve-figures don't witness the Unhoured until interacted with (see witnessed-loops, [01](01-game-design-document.md) §4.5); Now-people find the Unhoured hard to hold in memory (fiction cover for silent-protagonist strangeness, and it *hurts* correctly by endgame); the Hush does not dissolve them — it *defers*.

## 1. Bellkeeper Ambrose — the hub keeper

- **Role:** Mentor, hub NPC (the Vestry), tutorial voice, and the story's guilt engine — the master who surrendered his apprentice to the Order and has spent sixty-one years maintaining the bell that broke them.
- **Presence:** Blind, huge-handed, always mid-task on some corroded mechanism. Never sits.
- **Gimmick:** his *mind* is unstuck though his bodies are not. The three Ambroses are one continued conversation: ask Eve-Ambrose a question and Now-Ambrose may answer it unprompted; Hush-Ambrose — a figure half-fused into the Vestry wall, still speaking — answers questions the player hasn't asked yet. Implementation: one dialogue pool with cross-state continuation tags — a writing trick, not a systems cost.

| State | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Eve** | Master Bellkeeper at his height, apprentice-proud, on the eve of the choice. Doesn't know. Almost. |
| **Now** | The Vestry keeper. Kind, evasive, endlessly maintaining. Recognizes what you are immediately; says nothing for two acts. |
| **Hush** | Fused into the wall beside his bell, eyes open, voice intact. The only Hush-figure that speaks. *"Here is fine. I rang it from here."* |

- **Arc:** the confession can't be extracted, only *witnessed* — the Ossuary's coat forces it. Post-confession he keeps working; the coda changes. Whether the player forgives him is expressed only through where they choose to rest.

## 2. Sister Vesper of the Escapement — the pursuer

- **Role:** Recurring duelist, the law with a face, and thread 2's axis. The Escapement's finest reader of temporal deviance — assigned to you because she's the only one who can track an unwritten person, which is precisely what starts unwriting her certainty.
- **Gimmick:** **one self per state, three different women** — the roster's cleanest demonstration that the shards are parallel lives, not a timeline.

| State | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Eve** | A novice archivist in the Census, the night before it burns — doubting, kind, about to choose the Order out of fear. Docile loop; if *witnessed*, her loop breaks differently than any other Eve-figure's: she follows you for a while. Watching. |
| **Now** | The inquisitor. Iron, exact, devout, increasingly annotated. Duel #1 (Clockrow), duel #2 (Terraces, interruptible via thread 2). |
| **Hush** | **The Threnodist** — the Choir's prophet, a future echo leaked backward: what Vesper becomes after the certainty fails and only mercy-as-surrender remains. |

- **Arc:** the game's fullest character journey — zealot → reader → (player-dependent) ally at the Lantern, Custodian boss, or the third thing: the Threnodist, early. Ally-Vesper at the finale is the emotional payoff of the whole second act; make players earn it through thread 2, not affinity points.

## 3. "Thread" Mara — the slip-runner who cannot slip

- **Role:** Stitchguild handler, Tier II grantor, fissure cartographer, thread 1's heart. Dry, transactional, funny in the way of people guarding one soft spot with their whole body.
- **Gimmick:** **exists in the Now only, and is fray-blind — the one major NPC who cannot slip and never will.** She maps doors she can't open, for smugglers who can. Her family has been Resident in the Eve since she was nine; she budgets viewing-hours the way addicts budget.

| State | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Eve** | Not present as herself — but her *family* is: a market-day loop in the Tolls, a nine-year-old gap in it where she should be standing. The player can stand in the gap. She will never see it. |
| **Now** | Fully present. The only main-cast NPC who is *simply a person* — which, in this roster, is its own kind of haunting. |
| **Hush** | Absent. If thread 1 ends badly, her Guild-token turns up at the floodline. It is not explained. |

- **Arc:** thread 1. Her ask, eventually, plainly: *one real thing out of the Eve* — bread her mother baked, carried out in the Anchored Satchel, staling in real time in her hands. Whether that mends or ends her depends on the thread's close.

## 4. Magistrate Orven the Twice-Gilded — the winner

- **Role:** Terraces quest-giver, merchant of last resorts, thread 1's buyer, and the argument-embodied: what winning Carillon's game purchases. Not a villain. Worse: a receipt.
- **Gimmick:** **unstuck *upward*** — three centuries of purchased clean time have made him ageless and unmoored; he experiences his own life as an archive he visits. Perfect recall of transactions; faces, seasons, and grief have depreciated off the books.

| State | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Eve** | Present — he was alive then and is alive now, the only Now-person with a *true* Eve-self. His Eve-loop is him signing the Residency scheme's founding ledger. He knows it's there. He has never gone to look. |
| **Now** | The oldest private citizen in Carillon; speaks to the player as the first *new* thing in decades. Buys Resonance, sells centuries' accumulation, keeps bidding for your sternum-shard with escalating, horrifying politeness. |
| **Hush** | His gardens outlast everything (wealth dissolves last); a chair is set at the fog's edge, facing down-city. Waiting rooms are a lifelong habit. |

- **Arc:** thread 1's endpoint. If installed in the Eve, his epilogue is one line, delivered smiling, in the loop: *"At last. Nothing owes me anything."*

## 5. Hollow-of-Bells — the erased knight

- **Role:** Companion-at-intervals (summonable at select Custodians, Solaire-pattern), thread 3's axis, and the lore key that reframes the Order's crime from *event* to *foundation*.
- **Gimmick:** **exists in no state.** A Remnant of the Vigil of the Ford, erased in the Concordance; he flickers at the edge of all three shards and is fully solid only near the Unhoured. Two unwritten things, mutually visible: the game's warmest relationship is between its two least-existent characters.

| State | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Eve / Now / Hush** | The same grey figure at his post in all three — because a bridge that never existed decays in none of them. The one *constant* NPC, achieved by not existing. Set-dressing near him subtly disagrees with itself across states; his post is the only place it doesn't. |

- **Arc:** thread 3 — four traces, the Ossuary shelf, then the choice: fade *witnessed* holding proof, or be anchored — permanent, real, and the last Vigil knight in a world that will never make another. His summon-sign at the final Custodian appears regardless of choice; how solid he is when he answers depends on it.

## 6. The Anchorite — the one who holds

- **Role:** Final presence; not truly a boss fight so much as a boss *decision* (a combat phase exists if the player forces it — the game does not require it). The first Bellkeeper of the Wellfound Age, wound into the Meridian's resonant chains at the Fracture to hold the three shards apart.
- **Gimmick:** **all states at once.** Rendered as three superimposed silhouettes breathing out of phase; speaks in three tenses simultaneously (single voice line, triple-processed — an audio problem, deliberately, not an animation problem: MetaSounds earns its keep, see [05](05-ue5-technical-design.md) §8).

| State | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **All** | The Cell is the one room in Carillon where slipping does nothing: the player arrives in all three states simultaneously and the HUD indicator breaks — the mechanic itself is suspended as the final statement of place. |

- **Arc:** none. The Anchorite is past arc — a person spent down to a function plus a courtesy. Offers the situation, the hammer, and no counsel. Their last line is the same in all three endings: *"Thank you for coming back."*
